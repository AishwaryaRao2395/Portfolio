AT A GLANCE

I explored a mobile-first digital payments experience that combines onboarding, KYC verification, wallet access, utility payments, transaction history, and support into one product journey.

The core idea was progressive unlock: helping users begin with limited but meaningful functionality and gain access to more features as trust and verification milestones are completed.

Instead of treating compliance as a separate technical layer, I made it a visible part of the journey so the product feels honest, guided, and predictable.

Context

CONTEXT AND DESIGN LOGIC

Financial products have to balance speed and simplicity with security, trust, and compliance. In many payment apps, users only discover restrictions after they attempt something, which creates confusion and friction.

I structured the app around three major states:

  • Pre-KYC users with limited access and guided onboarding.
  • Users who completed Video KYC and can access wallet functionality.
  • Fully approved users who can use a wider set of payment and account features.

This state-based structure became the backbone of the product. It allowed the interface to change meaningfully depending on user status, rather than simply locking buttons without explanation.

I approached onboarding as both a registration journey and a trust-building journey. Multiple KYC paths made the flow feel more realistic and gave users a stronger sense of agency.

After KYC is submitted, the experience does not simply stop. The product communicates that the user is in a transitional state, what review may look like, and what they can still do while waiting.

The wallet becomes a central value layer, but not one automatically available to everyone. If the user is not ready, the reason is explicit. If they are eligible, the wallet becomes a natural bridge into payments.

The payment side is grounded in practical categories like electricity bills, mobile recharge, DTH or cable, and FASTag, making the product feel familiar and useful.

The strongest design decision is that access restrictions are not hidden. Users may not be able to do everything immediately, but progression stays understandable and motivating rather than frustrating.

FROM CLIENT WORK TO OPERATIONS

Photography and wedding studios do not operate like standard teams using generic project software. A single client project can include multiple events, photographers, deliverables, and staggered payment milestones.

The core problem was not simply how to list projects. It was how to turn booked client work into a structured, trackable operational flow.

I designed the experience as a connected journey:

  • Create the project and define the core client details.
  • Break the project into shoot events.
  • Plan deliverables.
  • Track payments and project progress together.

The Projects dashboard combines summary-level business visibility and project-level execution insight. It becomes a decision surface, not just a list.

The project summary panel adds richer operational context with reminders, completion states, payment information, and deliverable progress, reducing the need to bounce between screens.

The New Project flow uses four steps that reflect the real way studio work is scoped:

  • Project name and package price
  • Shoots
  • Deliverables
  • Client payments

The Shoots screen becomes the core planning layer, supported by filters like Unscheduled, Unassigned, Upcoming, and Today, plus detail panels for venue, timing, crew, notes, and attachments.

FROM FRAGMENTED FLOWS TO PARTICIPATION

Web3 products often ask users to switch between multiple contexts just to understand what they hold, what they can do next, and how participation works. I designed a more complete product surface where users can monitor assets, take action, and engage with governance from one place.

I organized the platform around four core workflows:

  • Overview
  • Transfers
  • Staking
  • Governance

The design direction centered on turning protocol capability into product clarity. Instead of exposing raw technical behavior, the interface turns balances, proposals, staking behavior, and transfers into a coherent dashboard-led experience.

One of the strongest aspects of the project is the visible progression from low-fi wireframes to a more resolved visual system with stronger hierarchy and a reusable design language.

The overview screen acts as the user’s anchor, surfacing available balance, asset distribution, portfolio summaries, and recent transactions in one place.

Governance became one of the most distinctive modules. Instead of treating voting as a shallow toggle, I designed for proposal listing, proposal detail, participation status, and outcome visibility.

Staking and transfers extend the platform from visibility into action, making it feel like a place to view, act, and participate, not just store tokens.

Context

STRUCTURING A DENSE ENTERPRISE SYSTEM

Data governance work is often fragmented across tools and teams. Data catalogs, access approvals, and compliance evidence frequently live in separate systems, which creates operational overhead and weakens visibility.

I treated the catalog and dataset layer as the foundation of the platform. If users cannot understand what data exists and how it is organized, the rest of governance becomes abstract.

On top of that foundation sits the policy layer. Policies are not just forms here. They are operational rules that can be created, mapped, previewed, edited, and simulated before activation.

Access management became another major pillar through users, groups, invitations, permission levels, and access review behaviors. Governance is ultimately about who can do what, under which conditions, and how those decisions are maintained over time.

Audit and compliance workflows extend the product into logs, violations, evidence, and reporting, turning the platform into a control surface rather than a setup-only tool.

I positioned AI as a workflow assistant for tasks like policy creation, catalog assistance, search, and schema conflict handling, supporting complex work without replacing governance logic.

FROM REAL-TIME RESPONSE TO OPTIMIZATION

Ground support operations at airports involve a large network of vehicles and equipment operating under strict timing pressure. Pushback tractors, GPUs, baggage loaders, ambulifts, fuel bowsers, and service vehicles all influence turnaround reliability.

I designed the monitoring dashboard to behave like a control-room interface. It uses a map-based foundation so users can understand assets in operational context rather than through abstract rows or isolated metrics.

The system supports multiple airports and operational zones such as service roads, emergency services, cargo operations, terminal operations, passenger services, and fuel operations. These zones make monitoring more meaningful because alerts can be understood spatially.

The operations dashboard moves up a level from incident response to fleet intelligence. It looks across time, asset categories, and airports to show recurring failures, maintenance categories, cost movement, SLA adherence, and utilization quality.

The concept becomes especially strong when it moves beyond diagnosis into recommendations. Instead of stopping at what is wrong, the dashboard suggests predictive maintenance, smart redeployment, and AI-driven allocation models.

That turns the interface into a decision-support tool rather than a passive reporting layer, and helps teams act, prioritize, and improve over time.
